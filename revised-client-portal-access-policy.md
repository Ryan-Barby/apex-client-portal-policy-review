# Apex Allied Health — Client Portal Access Policy (revised draft)

> Proposed policy for the fictional Apex Allied Health scenario, produced as an AI-tutor-assisted exercise. It is not evidence that the controls described are implemented.

## 1. Purpose and scope

**Purpose**

This policy sets out how Apex Allied Health manages staff access to the Apex Client Portal. Its aim is to ensure staff have the access they need for their duties, while limiting unnecessary access.

**Scope**

This policy applies to staff access to the Apex Client Portal, including administrator access. It covers access when staff join, change roles or leave, temporary access, and regular access reviews.

It does not cover access to other Apex systems.

## 2. Access principles and approvals

- Access must be based on the duties of the staff member's role and the requirements of their work.
- Staff must not be given more access than they need to perform those duties.
- The Practice Manager must approve access for other staff before it is granted or changed.
- If the Practice Manager is unavailable, the designated backup may approve access within the authority Apex has assigned to them.
- No person may approve their own access. Apex must designate another authorised person to approve and review the Practice Manager's access.
- If no authorised approver is available, the access request must wait.
- Administrator access must be limited to authorised tasks that require administrator permissions.
- Access decisions and changes must be recorded.

## 3. Role-based access

Apex must maintain a role-access table that defines the Client Portal access normally required for each role. The table must be based on the duties of each role and the portal's available permissions.

Apex must also maintain an individual access record showing each staff member's assigned role and Client Portal access. Access changes must be approved before they take effect, in accordance with Section 2.

The Practice Manager or an authorised administrator must update the relevant records and document what changed, who approved and implemented the change, when it took effect, and why.

Where someone needs access outside their normal role, the exception process in Section 4 must be followed.

## 4. Access exceptions and administrator access

Access outside a person's normal role must be approved before it is granted, in accordance with Section 2. The request and approval must be recorded, including the person's name and role, the requested access, the business reason, who approved it, and when the decision was made.

Before approving administrator access, the approver must confirm that the task requires administrator permissions and that a lower level of access would not be sufficient. Where the portal supports separate accounts, administrator tasks must use a separate, individually attributable privileged account rather than the person's everyday account.

Temporary administrator access must have an agreed start and expiry date and time. The Practice Manager — or, if unavailable, the designated backup — is responsible for ensuring access is removed by expiry. An authorised administrator may carry out the removal. The person who removes access must record who did so and when.

Any extension must be approved and recorded before the existing expiry time. If no authorised approver is available, access must be removed at expiry and the extension request must wait.

After expiry, the Practice Manager — or the designated backup if the Practice Manager is unavailable — must ensure the access is checked as soon as practicable to confirm it has been removed. The result, date, time and checker must be recorded in the exception record. If access remains active, the Practice Manager or backup must arrange its immediate removal and record the action taken.

No one may approve their own request for additional access. Apex must identify and authorise someone other than the Practice Manager to approve the Practice Manager's access.

## 5. Staff departures and role changes

**Departures**

The Practice Manager is responsible for ensuring a departing staff member's Client Portal access is disabled.

For a planned departure, access must be disabled by the end of the staff member's final working period. For an unexpected departure, access must be disabled as soon as practicable after the departure is confirmed. If there is a security concern, urgent action must be taken.

The person managing the departure must notify the Practice Manager. The Practice Manager, or an authorised administrator acting on their request, must arrange for the account to be disabled.

The Practice Manager is responsible for ensuring completion is checked and recorded. The person who disables the account must record when the action was taken. Where practical, a different authorised person must verify that the account is disabled. If an independent check is not practical, the person who disabled the account must perform and record an immediate self-check; this does not count as independent verification. A different authorised person must review and verify the change as soon as practicable afterward.

For a planned departure, the check and record must be completed by the end of the staff member's final working period. For an unexpected departure, they must be completed as soon as practicable after the account is disabled. Any security concern requires urgent action.

**Role changes**

A staff member's access must be reviewed before their role changes. Access that is no longer required must be removed, and any access needed for the new role must be approved and in place by the time the role change takes effect.

If this cannot be completed in time, the Practice Manager must document the reason and ensure the staff member does not retain access that is no longer required.

If the Practice Manager is unavailable, the designated backup is responsible for coordinating the required action. Apex must identify and authorise that backup in advance.

## 6. Access reviews

The Practice Manager, or a designated reviewer, must review Client Portal access monthly. The reviewer must compare the current portal account list and permissions against the current staff list, the role-access table and approved exceptions.

Any differences must be recorded and investigated. Before deciding whether access is inappropriate, the reviewer must check whether a valid exception has been approved. Differences must be referred to the Practice Manager for a decision if someone else is conducting the review.

Where a correction is required, the Practice Manager must arrange for an authorised person to make the change promptly, taking account of the risk. The action and completion date must be recorded.

The portal must be checked again to confirm the change was made. Where practical, someone other than the person who made the change should verify it. If an independent check is not practical, the person who made the change must perform and record an immediate check; this does not count as independent verification. A different authorised person must review and independently verify the change as soon as practicable afterward. If the independent verification has not yet occurred, the record must show that it is outstanding.

The reviewer must not be the sole person checking their own access. Another authorised person must review it against the approved records. Apex must identify and authorise who will approve and review the Practice Manager's access.

The date of each review, the reviewer, differences found, decisions, actions and verification must be recorded in an access review log. Monthly reviews do not replace action required when someone leaves, changes roles or reaches the expiry of temporary access.

## 7. Records and responsibilities

Apex must maintain the following records to support this policy:

- **Role-access table:** the access normally required for each role.
- **Individual access record:** each staff member's assigned role, approved portal access and relevant changes.
- **Exception record:** the business reason, approval, access granted, any expiry or extension, and confirmation of removal.
- **Leaver checklist:** notification, account disabling and completion checks.
- **Access review log:** review dates, reviewers, differences, decisions, actions and verification.

These records may be kept as separate documents or as clearly identified sections of a shared register. Access to them must be limited to people who need them for their responsibilities.

Apex must nominate and authorise a backup to coordinate access actions when the Practice Manager is unavailable. The Practice Manager and backup must have access to the records they need. Reminders must be set for temporary-access expiries, and upcoming actions must be handed over before a planned absence.

Apex must identify and authorise someone other than the Practice Manager to approve and review the Practice Manager's own access. If that person has not yet been identified, this must remain an open implementation item.

If an extension to temporary administrator access is needed while the Practice Manager and backup are unavailable, it must be referred to another authorised approver, if one has been nominated. Otherwise, access must be removed at expiry and the request must wait.

## Open implementation items

Before this proposed policy could be operationalised, Apex would need to determine:

- Who is authorised to approve and review the Practice Manager's access.
- Who the designated backup is and what authority they have.
- Whether another authorised approver is available for escalation.
- What the portal's Standard and Administrator permissions allow.
- Whether the portal supports separate privileged accounts and timed expiry.
- How portal access changes will be checked and how supporting records will be maintained.
